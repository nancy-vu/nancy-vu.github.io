### General Overview of the Program

The Justice Next Cohort is a "skill-building and leadership development 9-week program to empower and equip future leaders" (see [reference](https://dream.org/justice-next-cohort/)). The cohort is made up of BIPOC individuals between 18 to 24 years of age in Maricopa County (or the greater Phoenix area). The central issue areas of interest are at the insersections of criminal and environmental justice, while allowing room for exploration of other related issues (e.g. migration, education, etc).

This is the second time that the Justice Next Cohort is being hosted in Phoenix, Arizona. Last spring, it was hosted in Sacramento, California. 

This year's cohort involved mostly youth from the various colleges across the state, while also involving some formerly incarcerated youth and early-career adults. I myself am someone who studied out-of-state and recently returned to stay with my family. I am currently looking for work.

## My Overall Thoughts and Feelings

I think we have to remember how power and privilege function so that we do not lose ourselves in the process of trying to write a success story for our families. Success can mean many things, and subscribing to one model of success when we ourselves were never set up for the normative model of "success" is dangerous. I do not claim that we should abandon the effort to be seen as successful according to the mainstream defintions of success--- I think we all deserve the spotlight for all the things we have overcome to be where we are today. Rather, I want to remind us to never flatten ourselves by striving for success and shedding the less "successful" or attractive parts of our lives that have built us up for this success in the first place. We mustn't forget what the struggle was for. We also mustn't settle for success when the fight is never over until we all get to have a turn to speak and be remembered even when we are not the picture-perfect success story. 

I think we can strive for more than success. By that, I mean that we can transform what success means and looks like for all those that come before and after us. We have to remain committed to rebuilding all these structures from "failures" and "successes", to other definitions and categories that contain our complexity in boxes to be flattened and shipped elsewhere to sit on shelves and collect dust. I believe that within us we all carry different forms of wisdom and knowledge(s) (refer to Donna Haraway's texts) that are fragments of the broad history of Us. When we connect and make meaningful efforts to listen and understand, we get closer to piecing together our history, how our pathways have intersected and diverged, and how they have all contrbiuted to a system greater than the institutions that have tried to delineate the borders that cross our stories and divvy them into ileligible shards. It is difficult work, but nobody else will pick up the pieces for us--- it will prick at our skin, cut deep into our flesh, and leave a scar but at the end of the day, we will all bleed the same and have the same pain we want to continually heal for generations to come.

I hope we can remember this and continue to do the work of remembering and telling stories as a part of our survival and climb up.
